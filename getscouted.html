1

Kids born in the same year can be up to six years apart in physical development

In a Scottish FA-commissioned study of academy players, some boys in the same age group were up to six years apart biologically. One 15-year-old had the body of an 18-year-old. If your child looks small next to teammates, birth year may be the wrong comparison.

2

Academies tend to pick the early developers, and the gap grows every year

A 2025 study of 1,011 players at 12 Club Academy Scotland academies found that selection favoured early-maturing boys from under-12s onwards. The bias was small at U12 and large by U18. England shows the same pattern: at one English Premier League academy, 55% of U9 to U16 players were born September to November and only 13% June to August.

3

Late developers are the players most likely to slip through

Researchers found late developers were heavily underrepresented in Scottish academies. Many of them catch up physically later on. According to the Scottish FA's own guide, by age 14 no late-maturing players were left in the Club Academy Scotland system. Knowing which group your child is in helps you read a trial result for what it really is.

4

Academy players get screened. Most others never do.

The Scottish FA has introduced growth and maturation screening in Club Academy Scotland. Clubs submit maturation data in quarterly windows, and the results decide whether a player can play in a younger age group. Players outside academies, where most late developers end up, don't get this.

5

You can check it at home in five minutes, with no X-ray

The report uses the Khamis-Roche method, published in the journal Pediatrics and the same method used in Scottish academy screening. All you need is a tape measure, bathroom scales and both parents' heights.

Questions parents ask

How accurate is it?

In the original study, 9 in 10 predictions landed within 5.3 cm of actual adult height for boys and 4.3 cm for girls. It was developed on healthy children in the United States, so treat it as a well-founded estimate. Careful measurements make the biggest difference, and the order page shows you how.

What do I need to measure?

A tape measure, a bathroom scales, your child's date of birth and both biological parents' heights. Measuring takes about five minutes, and the order page walks you through it step by step.

I don't have a tape measure to hand. Can I still order?

Yes. Choose Silver and use the buy now, measure later link. We email you a private link straight away, and your report arrives minutes after you enter the measurements.

Will it tell me if my child will make it?

No. Height and maturity are one part of the picture, and plenty of top players were late developers. The report helps you understand why your child might look ahead of or behind teammates right now, and what is likely to change.

Is this medical advice?

No. It's an educational estimate. If your child has ongoing pain, very fast or very slow growth, or a condition affecting growth, speak to your GP.
