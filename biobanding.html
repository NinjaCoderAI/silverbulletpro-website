1

Kids born in the same year can be up to six years apart in physical development

In a Scottish FA-commissioned study of academy players, some boys in the same age group were up to six years apart biologically. One 15-year-old had the body of an 18-year-old. If your child looks small next to teammates, birth year may be the wrong comparison.

Same age group, very different bodies
2

Scottish academies tend to pick the early developers, and the gap grows every year

A 2025 study of 1,011 players at 12 Club Academy Scotland academies found that selection favoured early-maturing boys from under-12s onwards. The bias was small at U12 and large by U18.

Bias towards early developers, by age group
3

Late developers are the players most likely to slip through

Researchers found late developers were heavily underrepresented in Scottish academies. Many of them catch up physically later on. Knowing which group your child is in helps you read a trial result for what it really is.

~15%of boys are late developers, and they are the group academies miss most often.
4

The Scottish FA is now acting on maturity

After the research, the Scottish FA piloted a change that lets clubs request exemptions for more players to play in a different age group based on biological maturity. Maturity is now part of the conversation at clubs, so it helps to know where your child stands.

The report uses the Khamis-Roche method, published in the journal Pediatrics and the same method the Scottish study used to assess maturity. All you need is a tape measure, bathroom scales and both parents' heights.

It tells you when the growth spurt is coming

Around their fastest growth, many young players go through a clumsy spell and growth-related aches around the knees and heels. Seeing it coming means you can mention it to the coach early and help your player stay patient while their body catches up.

Questions parents ask

How accurate is it?

In the original study, 9 in 10 predictions landed within 5.3 cm of actual adult height for boys and 4.3 cm for girls. It was developed on healthy children in the United States, so treat it as a well-founded estimate. Careful measurements make the biggest difference, and the order page shows you how.

Will it tell me if my child will make it?

No. Height and maturity are one part of the picture, and plenty of top players were late developers. The report helps you understand why your child might look ahead of or behind teammates right now, and what is likely to change.

Is this medical advice?

No. It's an educational estimate. If your child has ongoing pain, very fast or very slow growth, or a condition affecting growth, speak to your GP.
