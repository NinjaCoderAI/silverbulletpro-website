Questions

Straight answers

How accurate is it?

The report uses the Khamis-Roche method, published in the journal Pediatrics in 1994 and developed on American children. In the original study, 9 in 10 predictions landed within about 2.1 inches of actual adult height for boys and 1.7 inches for girls. Treat it as a well-founded estimate, not a promise. Careful measurements make the biggest difference.

Don't teams already do this?

Some elite programs and academies measure growth and maturation. Most school, club and AAU players never get assessed, and their parents never see the numbers. That's why this report exists.

Will it tell me if my child will make it?

No. Height and maturity are one part of the picture, and plenty of top players were late developers. The report helps you understand why your child might look ahead of or behind teammates right now, and what is likely to change.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is an educational estimate. If your child has ongoing pain, very fast or very slow growth, or a medical condition affecting growth, speak to your pediatrician.

How often should I re-measure?

Every three to four months during the growth years. How fast your child is growing tells you more than any single measurement.
